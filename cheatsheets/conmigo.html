SPANISH PRONOUNS: CON...
★ A QUICK GUIDE TO SAYING "WITH ME, WITH YOU, WITH YOURSELF" ★
1 THE BIG THREE: CONMIGO, CONTIGO, CONSIGO

In Spanish, when the preposition "con" (with) meets the pronouns mí, ti, or sí, they fuse together into special, single words. You must never separate them.

con mí conmigo (with me)
con ti contigo (with you)
con sí consigo (with himself/herself/themselves)

Everyday Examples:

  • Ven conmigo.
    (Come with me.)
  • Quiero hablar contigo.
    (I want to speak with you.)
  • Ella llevó el libro consigo.
    (She took the book with her.)
2 THE REGULAR PRONOUNS

For all other pronouns, "con" stays separate like normal. No special fusions!

con élwith him
con ellawith her
con ustedwith you (formal)
con nosotros/aswith us
con vosotros/aswith you all (Spain)
con ellos/aswith them
3 THE GOLDEN RULE: "CON ÉL" vs. "CONSIGO"

Consigo is strictly reflexive. You ONLY use it when the person doing the action is doing it with themselves (the subject and the object are the same person).

If the subject and object are different, you must use con él, con ella, or con ellos.

Different People (Not Reflexive)
Fui al cine con él.
(I went to the cinema with him.)

Ella vive con nosotros.
(She lives with us.)
Same Person (Reflexive)
Él llevó el paraguas consigo.
(He took the umbrella with himself.)

Ellas trajeron comida consigo.
(They brought food with themselves.)
4 ADDING "MISMO" FOR EMPHASIS

We often add the word mismo after these pronouns to strongly emphasize the "self" (myself, yourself, himself). Note: "Mismo" acts like an adjective, so it must match the gender and number of the person it describes!

Singular Forms
conmigo mismo(a)with myself
contigo mismo(a)with yourself
consigo mismo(a)with himself/herself
Plural Forms
con nosotros(as) mismos(as)with ourselves
con vosotros(as) mismos(as)with yourselves
consigo mismos(as)with themselves

Estoy hablando conmigo mismo. (I am talking to myself - male speaker.)
Ellas estaban decepcionadas consigo mismas. (They were disappointed with themselves - female group.)

5 USEFUL EVERYDAY PHRASES
  • hablar conmigo mismo
    to talk to myself
  • estar contento contigo mismo
    to be happy with yourself
  • enfadarse consigo mismo
    to get angry with oneself
  • sentirse bien consigo mismo
    to feel good about oneself
  • aprender a vivir contigo mismo
    to learn to live with yourself
MEMORY TRICK: Why do they end in "-go"? In Latin, "with me" was mecum. Over centuries, this evolved into micum, and eventually migo. Spanish speakers forgot that "migo" already meant "with me", so they added "con" to the front!
Con-migo literally translates to "With-me-with"!
