SPANISH SUBJUNCTIVE CHEAT SHEET
★ QUICK GUIDE TO THE MOOD OF SUBJECTIVITY, DOUBT & EMOTION ★
1 WHAT IS THE SUBJUNCTIVE?

The subjunctive is not a tense; it is a mood. It reflects how the speaker feels about an action rather than stating a hard fact.

Indicative Mood Subjunctive Mood
Facts, certainty, reality, objective truth. Doubt, emotion, desires, subjectivity.
Formula: Subject 1 + Trigger + QUE + Subject 2 + Subjunctive
2 THE SUBJECT CHANGE RULE (1 VS. 2 SUBJECTS)

A core rule of the subjunctive: it is most commonly triggered when there are two verbs in a sentence, and each verb refers to a different person.

Indicative / Infinitive (Same Person) Subjunctive (Different People)
No me gusta aparcar el coche aquí.
I don't like parking the car here.		 No me gusta que aparques el coche aquí.
I don't like that YOU park the car here.
Quiero reducir la velocidad, voy demasiado rápido.
I want to reduce speed, I'm going too fast.		 Quiero que reduzcas la velocidad.
I want YOU to reduce speed.
Cuando estamos en la autovía, me encanta acelerar.
When on the highway, I love to accelerate.		 Cuando estamos en la autovía, me encanta que acelere.
When on the highway, I love that HE accelerates.
3 HOW TO CONJUGATE (PRESENT SUBJUNCTIVE)

The "Swap" Rule:
1. Go to the "Yo" form of the present indicative.
2. Drop the "-o".
3. Add the "opposite" endings.

For -AR Verbs (Use -ER/-IR endings)
yo-e (hable)
tú-es (hables)
él/ella/usted-e (hable)
nosotros-emos (hablemos)
ellos/ellas/ustedes-en (hablen)
For -ER/-IR Verbs (Use -AR endings)
yo-a (coma / viva)
tú-as (comas / vivas)
él/ella/usted-a (coma / viva)
nosotros-amos (comamos / vivamos)
ellos/ellas/ustedes-an (coman / vivan)

5 THE UNKNOWN VS. THE KNOWN (HYPOTHETICALS)

Use the subjunctive to express hypothetical statements, or when referring to someone/something you are not sure actually exists.

Subjunctive (Unknown/Hypothetical):
Busco a un hombre que sea sincero.
(I am looking for a man who is honest. — We aren't sure he exists; no specific person in mind.)

Busco a una chica que tenga una actitud positiva.
(I'm looking for a girl who has a positive attitude.)

If you are completely certain that a person or thing exists and are looking for them specifically, switch back to the indicative.

Indicative (Specific/Known):
Busco a una niña que tiene flequillo y un lunar en la cara.
(I am looking for a girl who has bangs and a mole on her face. — Like a parent looking for their specific lost child.)
6 CUANDO & PARA QUE
CUANDO + FUTURE = SUBJUNCTIVE

When the word cuando refers to an event that hasn't happened yet (the future), it triggers the subjunctive.

Cuando estés en casa, avísame.
(When you are home, let me know.)

PARA QUE = ALWAYS SUBJUNCTIVE

Whenever you use para que (so that / in order to), the following verb must be subjunctive.

  • Te lo digo para que lo entiendas.
  • Cierra la ventana, para que no haga frío.
  • Aprende portugués para que no tengas un choque cultural al llegar.
7 IRREGULAR VERBS (D.I.S.H.E.S.)

These verbs do not follow the normal conjugation rules. You simply must memorize them.

D - Dardé, des, dé, demos, den
I - Irvaya, vayas, vaya, vayamos, vayan
S - Sersea, seas, sea, seamos, sean
H - Haberhaya, hayas, haya, hayamos, hayan
E - Estaresté, estés, esté, estemos, estén
S - Sabersepa, sepas, sepa, sepamos, sepan
8 THE "BELIEF" RULE

INDICATIVE (Certainty / Declaring)

  • Creo que es verdad. (I believe that...)
  • Pienso que ella viene. (I think that...)
  • Es cierto que está aquí. (It's certain that...)
  • Es obvio que llueve. (It's obvious that...)
  • No dudo que lo sabes. (I don't doubt that...)

SUBJUNCTIVE (Doubt / Denying)

  • No creo que sea verdad. (I don't believe that...)
  • No pienso que ella venga. (I don't think that...)
  • No es cierto que esté aquí. (It's not certain that...)
  • Dudo que llueva. (I doubt that...)
  • Puede ser que lo sepas. (It may be that...)
9 ESCAPA CONJUNCTIONS (ALWAYS SUBJUNCTIVE)

When you see these connective phrases, the verb that follows them must always be in the subjunctive.

E - En caso de queIn case that
S - Sin queWithout
C - Con tal de queProvided that
A - A menos queUnless
P - Para queSo that / In order to
A - Antes de queBefore
10 CAR / GAR / ZAR

To preserve the original pronunciation of the verb, words ending in -car, -gar, and -zar undergo a spelling change.

-CAR -QUE Tocar toque
-GAR -GUE Jugar juegue
-ZAR -CE Almorzar almuerce
TIP: Don't let it overwhelm you. Remember that after "quiero que" (I want you to), the subjunctive always follows! Mastering the subjunctive is a simple path to success in mastering Spanish.
